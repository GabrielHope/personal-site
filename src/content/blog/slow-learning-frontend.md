I’ve been building this little site for longer than most people would consider reasonable.

There were days when I spent 4 hours just fighting with CSS variables, nav widths, and why `getStaticPaths` refused to see my posts. Days when I thought: “I could just copy a template and be done.”

But I didn’t.

Not because I’m a perfectionist (I’m really not), but because **rushing has never worked for me**.

Every time I tried to blast through React tutorials or “build 10 projects in 30 days” challenges, the knowledge evaporated within a week. Nothing stuck. I could follow along, but I couldn’t explain it to someone else. I couldn’t fix it when it broke. I couldn’t adapt it.

So I slowed down.

### What slow learning actually looks like for me right now

- One concept at a time.  
  Right now it’s Astro + content collections. I’m not jumping to React yet, even though everyone says I should. I want to **understand** static site generation, routing, and Markdown frontmatter deeply first.

- Writing while I learn.  
  Every time I figure something out (like why `post.render()` stopped working and how `render(post)` fixes it), I write it down here. Even if it’s messy. Even if only three people ever read it. Explaining forces clarity.

- Accepting “good enough” CSS.  
  This site will never be a design masterpiece. But it’s calm, readable, and loads in under a second. That’s enough for v1.

- No streaks, no pressure metrics.  
  Some weeks I write 0 lines of code. Some days I only read docs. That’s fine. Consistency over intensity.

### A small realization from the past few weeks

The internet is full of people who learned fast and got jobs quickly.  
That path is valid.  
But there’s also space for people who learn slowly, deeply, and quietly — and still end up in a good place.

I’m choosing the second path.

Not because I’m noble or anything.  
Just because the first path kept making me feel stupid, and this one makes me feel curious instead.

More notes as I go.

— Gabriel, January 2026
